Frequently asked questions

What is the full retirement age for someone born in 1952?

66. At that age, you receive 100% of your calculated benefit - no early-claiming reduction, no delayed-retirement credit.

What happens if I claim at 62?

Your benefit is permanently reduced to 75% of your full amount, since 62 is the earliest age Social Security retirement benefits can start.

What happens if I delay past my full retirement age?

Your benefit increases by 2/3 of 1% (8%/year) for every month you wait past 66, up to age 70 - reaching 132% of your full amount if you wait all the way to 70. There's no benefit to delaying past 70.

Where do I find my actual benefit amount?

This page shows percentages of your full benefit, not a dollar figure - your actual full-retirement-age benefit depends on your own earnings history. Look it up for free at ssa.gov/myaccount, then use the calculator to see your actual dollar amounts at every claiming age.
